# evidencelib v1.3.0 — release notes

Working notes for the v1.3.0 release and the SoftwareX software-update
article. `CHANGELOG.md` lists the same changes in short form.

**Theme.** Fusing several sources, some of them unreliable or nearly ignorant,
and comparing how combination rules and decision criteria behave. The release
was prompted by J. Dezert, who suggested adding PCR6+ because it keeps vacuous
sources neutral when more than two sources are fused.

**Principle.** Every method implements its original published definition and
is tested on the numerical examples of its source. No method is an alias of
another, even where two rules coincide in a special case. Known weaknesses of a
method are reproduced, not "fixed": the library exists to compare methods and
study their limitations.

## Summary

- **The complete PCR family:** PCR1–PCR6 and the improved PCR5+/PCR6+, all for
  any number of sources. Each conflicting product can be traced to see which
  proposition receives what.
- **Source handling and decision:** Shafer reliability discounting, the
  Jousselme distance, the DSmP transformation, decisions by BetP, DSmP,
  maximum belief, or maximum plausibility, and the TBM disjunctive rule.
- **Faithfulness fixes in existing rules:** `pcr5()` was an alias of `pcr6()`.
  Fusion results were silently trimmed and rescaled, which made Dempster's rule
  wrong under strong conflict. `dsmc()`, `yager()`, and `dubois_prade()` did
  not follow their definitions in edge cases.
- **Verification:** every new or changed method reproduces the numerical
  examples of its source paper (about 300 tests in total). An independent
  reimplementation agrees on hundreds of random cases.

## New methods

| Method | What it does | Source | Verified on |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pcr5_plus()`, `pcr6_plus()` | PCR5/PCR6 that redistribute each conflict only to the propositions that take part in it (binary keeping index); the vacuous source is neutral | Dezert, Dezert, Smarandache 2021 [DDS21], eqs. 23–26 | Examples 1–13, Tables I–XIV |
| `conflict_redistribution(rule=...)` | Lists, for each conflicting product, which proposition receives what under PCR5, PCR6, PCR5+, or PCR6+ | [DDS21] worked examples | Products π₁, π₇ (Ex. 2), Θ shares (Ex. 5), Example 13 |
| `pcr1()` … `pcr4()` | The earlier PCR rules: total or partial conflict, weighted by column sums or conjunctive masses; static and dynamic fusion (`model=`) | Smarandache, Dezert 2004 [SD04], eqs. 21–31 | Examples 7.1, 7.2, 12.1–12.5; Secs. 8–10 |
| `disjunctive()` | TBM disjunctive rule (masses combined by union) | Dubois, Prade 1986; Smets 1993 | Denœux 2008 [D08], Table 5 |
| `discount(a)` | Shafer reliability discounting | Shafer 1976; [SDT10] eq. 4 | [SDT10] Table 3 |
| `dsmp()`, `dsmp_of()`, `dsmp_regions()` | DSmP probabilistic transformation on Shafer, free, and hybrid models | Dezert, Smarandache 2008 [DS08], eq. 11 | All 9 examples, incl. PIC values |
| `jousselme_distance()` | Jousselme distance between two assignments | Jousselme et al. 2001 | Jiang 2017 [J17]: Examples 1–2, classic 20-value table |
| `decision(criterion)`, `decisions()`, `decision_scores()` | Decision by BetP (default, unchanged), DSmP, maximum belief, or maximum plausibility; `decisions()` reports ties | [DS08]; standard criteria | Unit tests |
| `from_json/from_csv/from_dict(..., exact=True)` | Restores a computed result exactly (no dropping, no rescaling) | — | Round-trip tests |

## Changes to existing methods

| Method | Before | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pcr5()` | Called `pcr6()`; two sources only | Own implementation of the general PCR5 formula; two or more sources | PCR5 = PCR6 only for two sources; PCR5 for S > 2 was missing. Two-source results unchanged. |
| All fusion rules | Results dropped masses ≤ 1e-9 and were silently rescaled | Returned exactly as computed | Dempster's rule returned `{B: 1}` instead of `{A: 0.45, B: 0.55}`, and raised "total conflict" at K = 1 − 9e-10 |
| `dempster()`, `pignistic*()` | Raised for conflict close to 1 | Raise only at total conflict; underflowing products handled exactly | Dempster's rule is defined for every K < 1 |
| `dsmc()` | Alias of `conjunctive()` on any frame | Raises `ValueError` outside the free DSm model | DSmC is defined on the free model only ([DS09] eq. 4) |
| `yager()` | Moved a source's m(∅) to Θ | Rejects sources with m(∅) > 0 | Yager's rule combines normal sources |
| `dubois_prade()` | Two sources, static only | k sources and `model=`; in a dynamic model change, lost mass stays lost (sum < 1) | Reproduces [DS09] §2.6.3 (result sums to 0.94), the known limitation that DSmH fixes |
| `pcr6()` | Skipped products whose masses summed to ≤ tolerance | Redistributes every conflict | The skip is not in eqs. 16–17 and lost mass once results keep small masses. Ordinary results unchanged. |
| PCR5/PCR6/PCR5+/PCR6+, DSmH, Yager, DP, measures | Accepted m(∅) up to the tolerance | Reject any m(∅) > 0 | These definitions require m(∅) = 0 |
| Pignistic, measures, fusion | Accepted assignments not summing to 1 and rescaled them | Raise `InvalidMassError`; `normalize()` is the explicit choice | Avoids silent renormalization of incomplete (dynamic DP) results |

**Breaking changes:** `dsmc()` on Shafer's/hybrid frames, `yager()` with
unnormalized sources, and fusion of incomplete assignments now raise. Use
`conjunctive()`/`smets()`, normalize first, or call `normalize()` explicitly.
Consider releasing as 2.0.0.

## Verification

- **Source examples.** Each method's tests reproduce the printed values of its
  source: [DDS21] Ex. 1–13, [SD04] Ex. 7.1–12.5, [DS08] Ex. 1–9, [SDT10]
  Table 3, [D08] Table 5, [J17] Tables 1–2, and [DS09] §2.6.3. The tolerance
  matches the printed rounding (1e-6, or 1e-4 where the source truncates).
- **Exact references.** The PCR tests include an independent rational
  (`Fraction`) transcription of the PCR5/PCR6/PCR5+/PCR6+ formulas. Dempster's
  rule is checked against exact arithmetic on random cases.
- **Independent review.** Codex (GPT-6.1-Sol) reviewed every change against the
  papers in several rounds and wrote its own implementations. They agree on all
  source examples and on 900 static and 900 dynamic random cases (maximum
  difference below 1e-15). All findings were fixed.
- **Quality gates.** 297 tests, 94% coverage, strict mypy, ruff, Sphinx with
  warnings as errors, and all examples run.

## Notes on the sources

Inconsistencies found while reproducing the papers. None affects the
implementation; each is documented in the tests.

- **[DDS21]:** Example 3 defines m₂(B) = 1 but writes m₂(A) in the product.
  Several values are mis-rounded by 1e-6, e.g. Table I C∪D 0.203385
  (exact 0.2033843).
- **[SD04] §10.5 (PCR4):** uses 0.5 instead of the column sum c(C) = 0.6 for
  A ∩ C. The printed A = 0.343636 and C = 0.310364 therefore do not follow
  the stated rule, which gives 0.33 and 0.324.
- **[SD04]:** two values are mis-rounded by 1e-6 (Ex. 12.1 PCR4,
  Ex. 12.3 PCR3/PCR4).
- **[DS08]:** values are truncated to four decimals rather than rounded
  (0.749254 is printed 0.7492).

## Questions for the authors (J. Dezert)

1. **PCR5 for more than two sources.** The general formula of [SD04] (eq. 33)
   and [DDS21] eq. 14, which `pcr5()` implements, give a share to every focal
   element of a conflicting product. The worked examples of [SD04]
   (Secs. 11.4–11.5) instead redistribute only to the canonical form of the
   conflict, and conclude that the vacuous source is neutral. With the same
   data, eq. 33/14 gives A∪B 0.185 instead of 0.05. Which is the intended
   PCR5?
2. **PCR5+/PCR6+ on DSm models.** [DDS21] defines them on Shafer's model. We
   apply them on free and hybrid models using DSm cardinality; the neutrality
   of the vacuous source still holds. Is this extension acceptable?

## Known limitations and interpretation choices

- **Dynamic fusion.** `pcr5()`/`pcr6()` (and PCR5+/PCR6+) have no `model=`;
  PCR1–PCR4, DSmH, Dubois-Prade, Dempster, and Yager do.
- **Dynamic PCR1–PCR4.** [SD04] gives only one numerical dynamic example
  (§7.2, PCR1). The rest follows its text, with documented interpretations:
  - the canonical form is taken over focal elements on the source model, then
    reduced in the target model;
  - total ignorance takes no proportional share in PCR2–PCR4;
  - degenerate conflicts go to u(X) (PCR1–PCR3) or X∪Y (PCR4), then Θ, else ∅.
- **Extensions to DSm frames.** The Jousselme distance and PCR5+/PCR6+ use DSm
  cardinality on free and hybrid frames; their sources define them on Shafer's
  model.
- **Not included.** Importance discounting ([SDT10] §4) needs the paper's
  adapted PCR5∅/PCR6∅ rules; it is left for a later release.
